[h1]Spellwoven Wands: Bringing Noita's Madness to Don't Starve[/h1]

Picture this: your Spark Bolt wand only knows how to shoot straight ahead. Then you slot Homing in front of it—and the next spark takes a turn and plants a kiss right on a spider's face. Add Explosion before that, and every hit becomes a little firework with absolutely no regard for friend or foe. Congratulations! You've learned this mod's first lesson: [b]the order of your spells IS your spell.[/b]

Spellwoven Wands brings Noita's "string spell cards together inside a wand" approach to Don't Starve. It's a [b]playground for combinations[/b]: the cards you own, their order, and your choice of wand decide whether your next move neatly harvests a patch of grass or sends you and your teammates sky-high.

[h2]Start with a Little Two-Slot Wand[/h2]

You start with a Spark Bolt wand by default. Equip it and right-click to fire. To craft your own spells, [b]build the Arcane Workbench first![/b]

At an [b]Alchemy Engine[/b], open [b]Prototypers & Stations / Magic[/b] or search these names:
[list]
[*][b]Arcane Workbench[/b]: 2 Boards, 2 Cut Stone, 4 Gold Nuggets, 2 Purple Gems. Grind gems into dust; craft wands and cards. Equip a wand nearby to install or rearrange cards.
[*][b]Weapon Engraving Table[/b]: 2 Boards, 2 Cut Stone, 1 Purple Gem. Turn blank cards, weapons/tools and recipe materials into spells. Install them at the workbench.
[*][b]Arcane Recycler[/b]: 2 Cut Stone, 1 Purple Gem, 4 Arcane Dust. Drop unwanted cards or wands beside it; right-click to recover dust. Store cards you want to keep first. Make its dust at the workbench.
[/list]
The workbench alone gets you started; add the others later. The wand editor's question mark opens the field guide.

Try Damage Plus before Spark Bolt for 10 extra damage. Compare wand slots, mana and cast delay. [b]Choose a non-shuffle wand to preserve your card order[/b]—a shuffle wand brings surprises!

[h2]Spells Aren't Just for Fighting[/h2]

This is the part I most want you to explore. The same engraving system can turn into a set of everyday survival tools:

[list]
[*]Throw a [b]Freshwater Fishing Rod[/b] toward a pond and let the fish come to your feet.
[*]Send a [b]Shadow Reaper[/b] through a row of grass. The scythe harvests along its path, with nearby grass going straight into your inventory when possible.
[*]Lob [b]Chum[/b] into the sea to attract fish, or a [b]Garden Hoe[/b] onto farmland to till it. Fishing and farming, all from your wand.
[/list]

When trouble arrives, throw a [b]Shadow Boomerang[/b] at it, or let a [b]Shield of Terror[/b] orbit you and block incoming blows. You can also prepare traps: Proximity Crystal Mines wait for enemies, Dormant Crystals can be detonated remotely, and Hollow Payload Eggs hold spells until a valid attacker cracks them open. [b]Your placements are saved with the world and remain there after you log off[/b]—though their activity pauses while the caster is away and resumes when they return.

[h2]Weird Things Worth Trying on Purpose[/h2]

[list]
[*][b]Giant Shot[/b]: enlarges both the visuals and actual reach of flying projectiles, static projectiles, explosions, and melee attacks, without increasing damage. One card gives 2.5× size, two give 5×, and three give 7.5×. Stars get larger light, temperature, and sanity-effect ranges too.
[*][b]Lifetime Inscription[/b]: extends flying-projectile lifetime, with [b]no upper time limit[/b]. Shorter Lifetime halves it per card, with multiple cards multiplying together. Fine-tune how far a shot travels or how long it orbits.
[*][b]Star Caller's Staff / Moon Caller's Staff[/b]: create a vanilla Dwarf Star or Polar Light at the release point. Movement and homing modifiers let them follow a selected target. Yes, you can make a star chase Deerclops.
[*][b]Collision and Timer Carriers[/b]: spells don't have to fire directly. Load melee attacks or explosions into a collision or timer carrier, and the payload goes off when the projectile hits or its timer runs out. [b]Even melee damage can be delivered right to an enemy's face.[/b] Piercing shots can release payloads on multiple hits; Shorter Lifetime lets you calculate where they'll go off. [b]A toy for people who like doing the math.[/b]
[/list]

[h2]Where Do the Cards Come From?[/h2]

Four ways:

[olist]
[*][b]Arcane Workbench inscriptions[/b]—blank cards plus materials. Check the recipes.
[*][b]Weapon Engraving Table[/b]—blank cards plus weapons or tools. For example: two spears + Nightmare Fuel + a blank card = Spear Thrust.
[*][b]Hunting creatures[/b]—spiders may drop Double Spell, adult MacTusks may drop Speed Up, and some bosses have exclusive drops.
[*][b]Collecting boss wands[/b]—some spells can only be found in loot.
[/olist]

Right-click spare cards together to make a pile of up to 25. Drop unwanted cards near an Arcane Recycler and right-click it to reclaim dust. Put any loose cards you want to keep safely in your inventory first!

[h2]A Few Handy Features[/h2]

The [b]Spell Test Punching Bag[/b] costs 3 Cut Grass and 1 Board at a Science Machine. Hits less than one second apart add to a running damage total, with full numbers displayed even above 9999. Test combos without volunteering your teammates as targets. Each player [b]within 12 units of their first boss victory[/b] earns an extra collision card: a 5% chance of Collision Trigger Modifier, or a 95% chance split equally among the collision variants of the six basic projectiles. No last hit required, no use of the normal card-drop allowance, and the record persists through character changes.

Language options are Auto / 中文 / English. Each player chooses independently; rejoin the world to apply changes. Right-click a wand with a Feather Pencil to rename it. A successful rename consumes one pencil; cancelling or keeping the same name costs nothing.
